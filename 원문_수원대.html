The obvious answer to the question how we know about the experience of others is that they are communicated to us, either through their natural manifestations in the form of gestures, tears, laughter, play of feature and so forth, or by the use of language. A very good way to find out what another person is thinking or feeling is to ask him. He may not answer, or if he does answer he may not answer truly, but very often he will. The fact that the information which people give about themselves can be deceptive does not entail that it is never to be trusted. We do not depend on it alone; it may be, indeed, that the inferences which we draw from people’s non-verbal behavior are more secure than those that we base upon what they say about themselves, that (A)__________ speak more honestly than (B)__________. But (C)__________ that we can rely a great deal upon words, we should know very much less about each other than we do.
