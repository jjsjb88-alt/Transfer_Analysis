It was also difficult to find enough available donors and surgeons when multiple patients required a transfusion at the same time. Around the same time, Oswald H. Robertson (no relation to Lawrence), a U.S. Army doctor, established the first blood depot: an ice chest stocked with flasks of blood. Roberston was sent to France to help the British army establish similar systems. He collected 0 negative blood (since it is the universal donor blood) and treated the blood with anticoagulants. It was then poured into one-liter glass bottles that were packed in straw in ammunition boxes, Schneid explains. The first successful transfusion from this early blood bank model took place in 1917.
