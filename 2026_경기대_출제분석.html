기출 본문 1/1Sufferers of Alien Hand Syndrome (AHS) experience involuntary but purposeful movements of one hand. Depending on the subtype, symptoms can include self-oppositional actions — undoing an action just performed by the unaffected hand — and repetitive and unwanted grasping of objects. Although AHS patients retain sensation in the rogue hand, they often perceive their hand as being an external entity acting of its own volition. The condition develops due to disrupted communication between specific brain regions. Under normal circumstances, the brain’s frontal lobe generates the signals for an intended movement and transmits these signals to the primary motor cortex, which executes the movement. For AHS sufferers, brain damage — particularly to the corpus callosum — means that the primary motor cortex is activated in isolation, leading to unintended physical movements.
