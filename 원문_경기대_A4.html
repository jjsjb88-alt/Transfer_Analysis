In 1753, the prolific collector Hans Sloane passed away. In his will, he asked the British government to use his collection to establish the first public museum, which would later become the British Museum. Sloane lived during a pivotal time in European intellectual history. As the British Empire’s trade network expanded, Sloane and others were able to travel the world and gather specimens and artifacts from far-flung regions. In keeping with traditions of the time, Sloane’s collection consisted of a hodgepodge of items ranging from animal skeletons to musical instruments. In the years after his death, the Enlightenment would take hold. A central tenet of this period was the systematization of knowledge, with attendant attempts to delineate and formalize distinct academic disciplines ranging from geology to anthropology. Because of this shift, Sloane’s assortment of items seemed haphazard and incoherent, harkening back to an era in which the public marveled at curious collections of strange objects of all types. Increasingly, it became the norm to treat objects as belonging to specific intellectual domains rather than to lump them all together. However, curators today are beginning to rediscover the value in breaking down disciplinary boundaries and juxtaposing seemingly disparate objects.

The stench was so intense in the (A)_________ summer heat that London’s public was driven to desperation. Because London was the world’s largest city and the center of a massive trading empire, no cost was spared on the system, which consisted of an elaborate series of tunnels, embankments, and pumping stations. When completed, the system diverted London’s human waste into a largely uninhabited stretch of the Thames downstream from London. As a result, London’s air quality improved, and incidences of water-borne diseases fell.

Randi Mann · The Weather Network · 2021-03-30 (현재 페이지 갱신2026-03-30)

원출처 ↗

Niagara Falls on the border between the United States and Canada is one of the world’s mightiest waterfalls. However, on March 30, 1848, the water completely stopped flowing over the falls. During the previous month, the weather became bitterly cold, and thick ice formed upriver on Lake Erie. Then, in March, a warm spell caused the ice sheet to break up. On March 28 and 29, high winds blew chunks of loose ice across the lake. The current drew these chunks into the mouth of the Niagara River, which feeds Niagara Falls. Eventually, the ice chunks piled up, creating a blockage between the cities of Buffalo and Fort Erie. The waterfall ran dry for around 30 hours. Since this incident, the “horseshoe” portion of the falls, which lies on the Canadian side of the border, has never ceased flowing. A structure called a boom is now installed on Lake Erie every winter. This structure regulates ice formation and ice flow, ensuring that water continues to flow over the Horseshoe Falls. The American section of the falls, which receives far less water than the Canadian side, was effectively shut off by the US government in 1969. This disruption lasted from June to November 1969 and was intended to facilitate research on the geology of the falls.

The first to try to decode the Maya script were the conquistadors themselves. One of their number, Diego de Landa, described the script as an alphabet, having assumed that its symbols were analogous to the phonetic symbols of the Latin alphabet. By the 20th century, however, the Ⓐ __________ view was that the script was a pictographic system - that is, each of its symbols stood not for a single sound but for a whole word or idea. Then, in the 1950s, a Russian linguist named Yuri Knorozov chanced upon a replica of a Mayan text. Approaching the symbols with a fresh pair of eyes, Knorozov concluded that there were too few to form a pictographic system but too many to form an alphabet. Instead, he realized, each of them represented a syllable, or a compound of vowels and consonants. While Knorozov's research met with resistance at first, it eventually came to be recognized as a pivotal contribution to the understanding of the script.

Then, in response to rapid technological progress, the public discourse surrounding unidentified flying objects (UFOs) shifted. In the late 19th century, when people reported unfamiliar objects in the skies, they often described exotic flying machines. In the early 20th century, as war loomed over Europe, discussion of UFO sightings again shifted. Fearing a German attack, many in Europe and North America blamed UFO sightings on German technology, pointing specifically to the recently developed Zeppelin, a large, gas-filled airship. These reports surged at the outset of World War Ⅰ. As time passed, new technologies continued to influence UFO reports and the public’s reaction to these reports. After the Nazis' defeat in World War Ⅱ, UFOs were often connected with Soviet rocket tests. Then, in the 1950s, as the Cold War deepened paranoia, the now-familiar notion that UFOs are alien technologies took hold of the public imagination.

Don’t blame adolescent social behavior on hormones

Bert Gambini · University at Buffalo News Center · 2018-03-19

the transition happened at the same age in both groups
원출처 ↗
시험에 실린 본문 읽기
Many of the behavioral changes associated with adolescence are widely attributed to the release of reproductive hormones during puberty. However, it has been difficult for scientists to disentangle the influence of puberty from that of adolescence on young people. While the two terms overlap significantly, they are also distinct. Puberty refers specifically to the process by which individuals develop reproductive functions, a process that occurs during adolescence. Adolescence, meanwhile, is a broad term that encompasses not only puberty-related changes but also a host of physical and psychological changes marking the transition from childhood to early adulthood. In order to isolate specifically puberty-related changes from general adolescence-related ones, researchers devised a carefully constructed experiment involving Siberian hamsters. These small rodents undergo puberty at different ages depending on when during the year they are born. Those born when daylight hours are long enter puberty at a younger age than those born when daylight hours are shorter. By controlling the amount of light that newborn hamsters received, the researchers bred two groups of hamsters: one that underwent puberty at around 30 days of age and another that did so at around 100 days of age. The researchers then observed whether behavioral changes occurred in response to the rodents approaching physical maturity or to the release of reproductive hormones during puberty. The researchers were specifically interested in whether the former group showed the shift from play to social dominance behaviors earlier than the latter group. Researchers found that the transition happened at the same age in both groups, independently of puberty. This led to the conclusion that reproductive hormones are unlikely to be the cause of at least some of the behavioral changes associated with adolescence.

All ants possess an acute sense of smell, enabling them to distinguish between numerous odors. Ants detect scents using their antennae, which are covered in a waxy coating composed of cuticular hydrocarbons (CHCs). These compounds serve primarily to stop the antennae from becoming dried out. Additionally, they help ants acquire important information from fellow colony members. When two ants touch antennae, each can smell the odor given off by the CHCs in the other’s antennae. In some ant species, this odor is affected by environmental conditions. For example, an ant that spends time foraging in the sun will come to smell differently from one that remains in the nest. This information can help certain ant species coordinate their actions for the benefit of the colony.

The Dangers of Reading in Bed

Nika Mavrody · The Atlantic · 2017-05-19

reading in bed became synonymous with irresponsibility, danger, and moral failing.
원출처 ↗
시험에 실린 본문 읽기
In 18th-century Europe, the practice of reading in bed was frequently frowned upon. Newspapers even went so far as to portray it as a dangerous hobby, highlighting house fires that had been caused by people falling asleep in bed without extinguishing the candles that they had been using for reading. Although such incidents were exceedingly rare, reading in bed became synonymous with irresponsibility, danger, and moral failing. Why was European society so troubled by reading in bed if it posed so little danger? Such reactions are common during periods of rapidly changing social norms. Before the 18th century, only those educated enough to read, wealthy enough to own books, and privileged enough to have personal space could consider reading a book in bed. As a result, reading was very much a communal activity for commoners, as was sleeping, since most families did not have the space to sleep separately. In the 18th century, this situation started to change. Standards of living improved, resulting in higher literacy rates, wider ownership of books, and greater space for sleeping alone—and reading in bed. As social norms were upended, many people naturally felt a sense of anxiety and reacted negatively.

Alien Hand Syndrome

Alan Bellows · Damn Interesting · 2005-11-19 (현재판 갱신2020-01-12)

they often perceive their hand as being an external entity
원출처 ↗
시험에 실린 본문 읽기
Sufferers of Alien Hand Syndrome (AHS) experience involuntary but purposeful movements of one hand. Depending on the subtype, symptoms can include self-oppositional actions — undoing an action just performed by the unaffected hand — and repetitive and unwanted grasping of objects. Although AHS patients retain sensation in the rogue hand, they often perceive their hand as being an external entity acting of its own volition. The condition develops due to disrupted communication between specific brain regions. Under normal circumstances, the brain’s frontal lobe generates the signals for an intended movement and transmits these signals to the primary motor cortex, which executes the movement. For AHS sufferers, brain damage — particularly to the corpus callosum — means that the primary motor cortex is activated in isolation, leading to unintended physical movements.

In 1947, A High-Altitude Balloon Crash Landed in Roswell. The Aliens Never Left

Donovan Webster · Smithsonian Magazine · 2017-07-05

what they were concealing was not an alien spacecraft
원출처 ↗
시험에 실린 본문 읽기
In June 1947, a rancher found a strange wreck in the desert near Roswell, New Mexico. He delivered parts of the wreckage to a sheriff in the city. The sheriff, in turn, notified an officer at Roswell Army Air Field. The wreckage was ultimately transferred to the Air Force base in Fort Worth, Texas. An initial press release from Roswell Army Air Field stated that the debris had originated from a “flying disk.” Although the US War Department soon after identified the wreckage as the remains of a fallen weather balloon, the media published sensationalistic headlines about a “flying saucer,” creating a lasting fascination with UFOs. After the news reports, many people assumed that the government was covering up the discovery of an alien spacecraft. They were partly correct. The government was hiding the truth, but what they were concealing was not an alien spacecraft but a classified espionage project called Project Mogul. As part of this project, the US government was sending high-altitude balloons to monitor nuclear testing in Russia. Each of these balloons was equipped with a long tail that carried various types of sensors and listening devices. Some of these balloons were being launched in the desert near New Mexico’s border with Texas.
